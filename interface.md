# Interface

*Interface*: Latin *inter*, between, and *facies*, face. The surface where a person and a system meet. It should show the work and little else.

The values below are Folio's. Another product keeps its own values and these rules.

## Bare

- Labels only where they are needed to use the thing.
- No callouts, subtitles, helper notes, taglines or flavour text. If a control needs a sentence, fix the control.
- No title repeats what the breadcrumb or the navigation already says. A section does not spend space restating its own name.
- What may stay: a real constraint, a consequence the person must know before acting, and a status.
- One place to change a setting. One save per view. One label per action.

## Layout

- A room holds exactly what it needs. A record opens beside the room, so the room stays uncluttered.
- Quiet chrome. Framed panels with a hairline, not shadows. Shadow belongs to floating layers only.
- Flat, grouped navigation. Groups are split by a rule. No nested trees.
- Spacing steps by 4. Equal things get equal sizes. Space inside a group is smaller than space between groups.
- Things line up: icons in one column, labels on one line, numbers right-aligned.
- Two control heights: 34 for main actions, 28 for compact ones.
- Nested radius: inner equals outer minus padding. Panel 12, control 8, chip 6.
- Use the space. Organise for the work, and waste none of it.

## Type

- One family for everything. Folio uses Instrument Sans. No serif partner.
- No monospace. Numbers use tabular figures in the same family, in one format per screen.
- A few sizes. Weights 400 and 500. Hierarchy comes from size and colour, not weight.
- Sentence case. No tracked uppercase labels.

## Colour

*Token*: Old English *tācen*, a sign. A token names a role, and the theme gives it a value.

- Views read tokens and never a raw colour, so another theme can sit beside the first.
- One neutral ramp carries surfaces, lines and text. Text has three working roles: ink, mid, muted.
- One accent, used rarely: the primary action, the selection, the number that matters. Clarity blue, unless the workspace owner picks another.
- Status colours only carry meaning, and sit inside the ramp.
- Two brand roles. *Arrive*, an emerald, marks an arrival, once per screen. *Clear*, a clarified white, is what a thing takes as it resolves.
- Depth comes from surface steps and hairlines. No hue gradients, gradient text, colour washes or decorative glow. Light is saved for the one primary action.
- Icons come from one set, at one size and stroke, in the colour of their text role. No emoji in chrome.

## Motion

- Motion explains a change. It is short and smooth, on one easing curve.
- Three signatures. The *flash*: something resolves. The *pour*: things arrive in order. The *turn*: the other side of the same thing.
- Numbers roll only when the value changes, never on load.
- Nothing loops unless it is live.
- Everything that moves has a reduced-motion version that shows the end state.
- An approved concept's motion is built as designed. Do not shorten it to fit a general rule.

## Behaviour

- Undo over confirm, for anything reversible.
- Autosave with a quiet "Saved". Edit in place, not in a modal.
- A loader waits before it shows, and a skeleton matches the final layout so nothing jumps.
- A side panel, not a modal, for what stays open while the person works.
- Every state is designed: loading, empty, error, too much data.
- A visible focus ring for the keyboard.

## Charts

- A smooth line, a live end dot, a value label that rides the line, eased changes between ranges.
- One accent per chart, on a muted grid. No glow and no gradient fill.
- A change says what it compares to.

## Phones

*Tactical*: Greek *taktikos*, of arranging. A phone screen is arranged for one move.

- A phone gets its own design. It is not the desktop made small, and it may differ in tabs and order.
- One job per screen, one main action, fewer words. Never dense.
- People start work from it easily.
- Targets are at least 40 tall. Text links get a 44 tap zone.

## Pictures

- App screens rarely need one. Their moments come from the product.
- Where a picture belongs, a public-domain painting or a real photograph comes before a generated image. Credit it quietly.
- Sample data is synthetic and plausible.

## Signs it is not finished

- Anything explaining the interface to the person using it.
- Gradients, glows, blobs, glass, sparkles for AI.
- Emoji as icons or decoration.
- Heavy weights and tracked uppercase.
- Pills and badges on everything.
- Hype words and exclamation marks.
- Read-only values styled as fields. A status dot with no label.
- A raw colour in a view.
- A screen that would fit another product with the name swapped.
