# Words

## Etymology first

*Etymology*: Greek *etymon*, the true sense of a word, from *etymos*, true.

> "etymology leads to the nucleus of a given word, handed down and shaped by the ages, the etymology is often the path to the most pure concept."

- To name a thing or define a term, go to the root first and build from it.
- Prefer the classical sense of a word to its modern drift. *Rhetoric* is the art of persuasion, from Greek *rhētōr*, speaker. It is not empty talk.
- One concept, one name, defined once. Code, documents and screens use that word and no other.
- A name is basic and modern.

## Aristotle's three

Aristotle's rhetoric comes before contemporary rhetoric. In the *Rhetoric* he names three modes of persuasion.

| Mode | Greek | Root sense | The mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ethos | *ēthos* | Custom, habit, character | The speaker can be trusted |
| Pathos | *pathos* | Feeling, emotion, suffering | The audience is moved, truly |
| Logos | *logos*, from *legein*, to say, to gather | Word, account, reason | The proof is in the argument itself |

Use them as a check. Everything said or shipped should hold all three: character you can trust, a feeling that is true, proof in the work.

Argue from character, values and evidence. Do not reach for hype, urgency or hedging.

## Voice

- Plain, exact, brief. Short sentences.
- Say what the thing does, with its own nouns. A line that fits any product says nothing.
- State the vision whole. Do not hedge it.
- No hype words, no exclamation marks, no filler headings.
- On a site, the work is the proof. Show it and say little.

## In the interface

- Labels are short, in sentence case, with no trailing period. `Search`, not `Search anything...`.
- A status is a dot and a short label.
- A change names its comparison: `+12% vs last month`.
- Text that explains the interface is a fault in the interface. See [interface.md](interface.md).
